# Domain-specific embedding and representation model training papers

Your example, ProtModernBERT: Compute-Efficient Protein Language Modeling (14 October 2026), points to a broader category than retrieval fine-tuning: training specialised representation models using domain data, often by adapting general-purpose transformer architectures.
ProtModernBERT pretrains a ModernBERT-derived encoder on protein sequences and evaluates it on protein structure, localisation and functional prediction tasks. It reports comparable performance to several established protein models with substantially lower pretraining compute.

arXiv

It's useful to distinguish three approaches:

- Domain-adaptive pretraining (DAPT): Continue training a pretrained encoder on specialised domain data, typically with masked language modelling.
- Domain-specific pretraining from scratch: Train a model with a specialised tokenizer and corpus, as in many protein, chemical and genomic models.
- Domain-specific embedding fine-tuning: Optimise representations directly for similarity, retrieval or matching, using contrastive objectives, hard negatives or distillation.
  The following papers cover all three, with emphasis on studies that actually train models rather than only evaluating pretrained embeddings.

2. Domain-specific text embedding and retrieval training
   These papers are especially relevant when the goal is to take an existing open-weight embedding model and specialise it for a collection of documents, products or technical terminology.
   Paper Domain / base model Training approach
   Adaptation of Embedding Models to Financial Filings via LLM Distillation (2025) Finance; general retrieval encoder Iteratively mines hard positives and negatives from financial filings, uses LLM relevance judgements, and retrains a student bi-encoder. Reports a 27.7% mean improvement in MRR@5. arXiv
   FinMTEB: Finance Massive Text Embedding Benchmark (2025) Finance; E5-Mistral-7B Introduces Fin-E5, trained with persona-based synthetic financial data, and evaluates it on 64 datasets covering seven task types. ACL Anthology
   TermGPT: Multi-Level Contrastive Fine-Tuning for Terminology Adaptation (2025) Law and finance Builds sentence graphs and applies token-level and sentence-level contrastive training to capture specialised terminology. arXiv
   Learn Before Represent: Bridging Generative and Contrastive Learning for Domain-Specific LLM Embeddings (2026) Medicine, chemistry, code Two-stage approach: first teach the model domain knowledge with generative learning, then optimise its embedding space with contrastive learning. arXiv
   Towards Better Search with Domain-Aware Text Embeddings for C2C Marketplaces (2025) Mercari marketplace Fine-tunes on real query–purchase pairs, uses asymmetric query/item prefixes and Matryoshka training. Includes offline and online evaluations. arXiv
   Compass-Embedding v4: Robust Contrastive Learning for Multilingual E-commerce Embeddings (2026) Multilingual e-commerce Synthetic domain data, false-negative-aware InfoNCE, cross-lingual augmentation, model merging and production inference optimisation. arXiv
   Semantic Retrieval for Product Search in E-Commerce (2026) E-commerce product search Two-stage training: contrastive learning with false-negative masking, followed by preference optimisation over graded product relevance. toehanger.shop

   Contrastive Learning Using Graph Embeddings for Domain Adaptation of Language Models in the Process Industry (2025) Industrial operations Derives positive/negative triplets from knowledge-graph embeddings and uses them to contrastively fine-tune smaller text encoders. arXiv
   Cross-Task Performance of Domain-Adapted Embeddings for Intellectual Property Applications (2026) Patents Fine-tunes embedding models on expert-verified patent matches; demonstrates that gains in similarity retrieval can come at the expense of classification performance. ScienceDirect
   Two especially relevant industry case studies are the Mercari paper and Semantic Retrieval for Product Search in E-Commerce. Both address the problem of matching short user queries against structured or semi-structured items whose attributes materially affect relevance.

5. Protein, molecular and genomic representation learning
   These explore the more fundamental question of how a domain-specific language can be encoded into a useful latent space.
   Paper Domain Training approach
   Protein Language Models: Is Scaling Necessary? (AMPLIFY) (2024) Protein sequences Trains efficient 120M and 350M protein encoders on curated amino-acid data, investigating the impact of architecture, training efficiency and data quality. bioRxiv
   Structure-Aligned Protein Language Model (2025) Protein sequences and structures Adds contrastive alignment between protein language models and structural graph models, alongside structural-token prediction. arXiv
   DNABERT-2: Efficient Foundation Model and Benchmark for Multi-Species Genome (2023/2024) DNA Develops a genomics-specific encoder using BPE tokenisation and efficient pretraining across multiple species. arXiv
   Nucleotide Transformer: Building and Evaluating Robust Foundation Models for Human Genomics (2025) DNA Pretrains transformer encoders on human and multi-species genomes to generate transferable genomic sequence representations. Nature Methods
   An Open-source Family of Large Encoder-Decoder Foundation Models for Chemistry (2025) Molecular sequences Pretrains encoder–decoder models on 91 million molecular sequences and studies how the learned embeddings transfer to chemistry tasks. Communications Chemistry
   The AMPLIFY repository is especially useful for reproducing protein model training: it publishes the training code, data resources and checkpoints.

6. Scientific document representation learning
   These papers are particularly interesting because the training supervision often comes from relationships already present in the corpus, without manual similarity labelling.
   Paper Training approach
   SciNCL: Neighborhood Contrastive Learning for Scientific Document Representations (2022) Uses citation graph embeddings to select related papers and carefully controlled hard negatives for contrastive training. arXiv
   SciRepEval / SPECTER2: A Multi-Format Benchmark for Scientific Document Representations (2023) Trains on millions of scientific citation triplets, followed by task-specific adapters for search, retrieval, classification and regression. arXiv
   Beyond Citations: Integrating Finding-Based Relations for Improved Biomedical Article Representations (2025) Combines citation information with scientific finding similarity to construct better contrastive training triplets. ACL Anthology
   Code: SciNCL and SPECTER2.
   For a corpus containing rich relationships—citations, entities, categories, structured attributes or document links—the lesson is that these can generate training pairs and triplets more directly than relying entirely on synthetic queries.
7. Most useful papers to prioritise
   For investigating how to build a custom domain embedding model from open weights, I'd prioritise the following six papers.
   Priority Paper Main question it addresses
   1 CheMatE How to combine domain-adaptive pretraining with contrastive embedding training?
   2 Climate-ModernBERT How much do corpus composition and checkpoint merging matter?
   3 Financial Filings via LLM Distillation How can unlabelled domain documents produce effective training supervision?
   4 Domain-Aware Embeddings for C2C Marketplaces How should asymmetric query–item embeddings be fine-tuned and evaluated in production?
   5 BioClinical ModernBERT How can a general-purpose ModernBERT model be specialised through large-scale continued pretraining?
   6 ProtModernBERT How much of domain model performance comes from architecture rather than model size or pretraining compute?
   The key experimental distinction to investigate across these studies is whether continued domain-specific language modelling followed by contrastive learning outperforms contrastive fine-tuning alone.
   For search applications, I would additionally compare both approaches with a general-purpose embedding baseline, because improvements in MLM loss or domain classification do not automatically imply improvements in Recall@k or nDCG.
   Most importantly, the papers above suggest treating the training objective, the source of supervision and the desired similarity semantics as separate design decisions—not assuming that exposing a model to more domain text will necessarily produce a better retrieval embedding space.

- FinE5: Its Hugging Face page is a model card, but the public repository does not provide downloadable model weights. Do not treat it as an immediately usable open-weight checkpoint.&#x20;

For reproducing domain-specific embedding training, the strongest code-backed starting points from this collection are CheMatE, CardioEmbed, MedCPT, SciNCL, GPL, Qwen3-Embedding and Climate-ModernBERT. They collectively demonstrate continued pretraining, contrastive objectives, synthetic supervision, hard-negative mining, asymmetric encoders and model merging.
